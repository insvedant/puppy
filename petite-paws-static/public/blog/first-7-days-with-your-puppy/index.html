Bringing Your Puppy Home: The First 7 Days

The first week sets the tone for everything that follows. Keep it calm, keep it simple and stick to a routine.

Before day one

  • A small crate or playpen with a soft bed
  • The same food your puppy is already eating
  • Puppy pads, an enzyme cleaner and a few chew toys
  • A lightweight harness (not a collar) for tiny breeds
  • A nutritional gel in case of low blood sugar

Days 1–2: Quiet arrival

Limit visitors. Show your puppy its bed, water and potty spot, then let it rest. Toy puppies sleep 18–20 hours a day, and that is normal. Some crying at night is also normal; a warm blanket that smells of its litter helps.

Days 3–4: Build the routine

Feed at the same times every day. Take your puppy to the potty spot after every meal, nap and play session. Praise every success, and clean accidents with an enzyme cleaner so the smell doesn't draw the puppy back.

Days 5–6: Gentle socialization

Introduce new sounds, surfaces and calm people in short sessions. Keep your puppy off public ground until its vaccinations are complete, but you can carry it outside to see the world.

Day 7: First vet check

Book a wellness visit within the first week. Bring the vaccination and deworming records from your puppy pack. This visit is also required to keep your health guarantee active.

Watch for these signs

Small breeds can develop low blood sugar. If your puppy seems weak, wobbly or very sleepy and won't eat, rub a little honey or nutritional gel on its gums and call your vet right away.
